Caring for a Parent With Dementia: Where to Start

Memory changes can feel overwhelming for a family. Here are a few simple, non-medical first steps. They are meant to go alongside your parent's doctor's advice.

By SBS Home CareOctober 29, 20262 min read

Start with the doctor

Questions about diagnosis, symptoms, and medicines belong with your parent's doctor. Bring a list of questions to each visit.

Find trusted support

The Alzheimer's Association has a free 24/7 Helpline at 1-800-272-3900. Your local Area Agency on Aging can also point you to programs in Connecticut.

Talk with patience

  • Speak calmly and use short sentences.
  • Ask one question at a time.
  • Give them time to answer.
  • Try not to argue. Listen for the feeling behind the words.

Keep routines familiar

Meals, rest, and activities at similar times can make the day feel calmer. Enjoy things they love, like music or old photographs.

Keep the home comfortable

Good lighting and clear walkways help. See our home checklist.

Plan together as a family

Talk about who can help with what. Many families also speak with an elder law attorney about legal and financial plans.

Where we can help

Our non-medical dementia support offers patient company and help with daily routines. Respite care gives family caregivers time to rest.

Common questions

Does SBS Home Care treat dementia?
No. We provide non-medical support only. We do not diagnose, treat, or manage any medical condition.
Where can families find help in Connecticut?
Start with their doctor, the Alzheimer's Association Helpline (1-800-272-3900), and your local Area Agency on Aging.
