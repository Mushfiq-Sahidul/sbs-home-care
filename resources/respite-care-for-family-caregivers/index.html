Respite Care: How Family Caregivers Can Take a Break

If you care for a parent, spouse, or relative, you may have put your own needs last. Taking a break is not selfish. It helps you keep going.

By SBS Home CareOctober 29, 20262 min read

What is respite care?

It is short-term, non-medical help. A caregiver spends time with your loved one while you rest, run errands, or go to your own appointments.

Why breaks matter

Caregivers often juggle work, family, and care. Time to rest and see friends is part of caring for yourself. If you are worried about your own health, talk to your doctor.

How to plan a good break

  1. Start small. A few hours is fine.
  2. Meet the caregiver first if you can.
  3. Write down the daily routine and favorite things.
  4. Agree on what is included.
  5. Decide how to stay in touch.
  6. Use the time to rest.

Where to learn more

Read about our respite care or call us. Your local Area Agency on Aging may also know of Connecticut programs.

Common questions

Is respite care medical care?
No. It is non-medical company and everyday help.
How long can a respite visit be?
It depends on your needs and our availability. Call us with your dates and hours.
